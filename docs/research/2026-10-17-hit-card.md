# The hit card: a black boar in the corner with a red dot where each shot landed

Task 198. Written before the code, per CLAUDE.md rule 1.

Karen, 2026-10-16 ~10:35, with a reference frame of somebody else's hunting video
(`<runs-dir>/193-refs/karen-ref-hit-silhouette.png`, LOOK REFERENCE ONLY -- never copied into the
repo and never uploaded):

> "and also save this one there is a black boar image as you can see and red dot / so once we hit
> boar we can show where it has been hitted / red dot will indicate so if shells then several of
> them / so somewhere in corner after hit we indicate where it landed"

## 1. What the system must do

1. The moment one of **my** shots lands on an animal, a small card appears in a screen corner.
2. It shows the animal **broadside, solid black**, and a **red dot** at each place that shot struck.
   A slug or a rifle bullet is one dot; buckshot is **one dot per pellet that hit**, and a pellet
   that missed is not drawn.
3. It appears within 0.3 s of the hit, holds about 3 s, fades.
4. Nothing for a miss. Nothing for a hit on a beater or on scenery. Nothing for somebody else's shot.
5. The fatal hit is marked the way the drive report already marks it.
6. Readable on a phone (400 x 800), in the Designer's approved UI style.
7. **Server-authoritative**: the dot positions come from the server's hit record. The client never
   decides where a pellet landed.

So **nothing about the picture or the projection is invented**. The one thing the game did **not**
have is per-pellet landing places: `Hits.group` deliberately collapses nine pellets into one report
with `pellets = 9`, because that is the contract `HitLog`, `Match` and the hit marker all read.
Karen's "if shells then several of them" needs the nine. That is the only new data in this task.

## 3. External sources

1. **theHunter: Call of the Wild / Way of the Hunter -- the "shot placement" card.**
   <https://www.thehunter.com/> , <https://www.wayofthehunter.com/> . Proprietary, both actively
   maintained (2026). *Good:* the convention this is -- a small animal diagram with the entry mark,
   shown right after the shot and again in the harvest screen, is how both games tell you where you
   hit. The player learns one picture and reads it in two places. *Bad:* both draw an anatomy
   diagram with organs the simulation models; this game has five zones and nothing else, and
   drawing a liver it never simulates is the mistake Way of the Hunter's own players complain
   about (already recorded in `docs/research/2026-10-05-drive-report.md`).
2. **Roblox `UICorner` + `Frame` composition**, <https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/UICorner>
   and <https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/frames>. Roblox-maintained, current. *Good:* a shape made
   of rounded frames at SCALE costs nothing, needs no upload (the asset tool here is models-only --
   an image cannot be uploaded from this machine at all) and redraws at any size. *Bad:* no
   transform: a `Frame` cannot be flipped, so a mirrored animal is a second layout, not a scale of
   -1. That is why `BoarArt.build` takes a `mirror` flag and lays the parts out as `u -> 1 - u`.
3. **WCAG relative luminance**, <https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG20/#relativeluminancedef>. W3C
   Recommendation, stable. *Good:* already adopted in this repo as `Report.ringColor` (task 137),
   and it is what says a near-black animal on a `#1C1915` card is not a picture of anything --
   luma 0.03 against 0.10. *Bad:* it says nothing about a 7 px dot's shape, only its separation.
4. **The Designer's `shop-design-spec.md` section 8, "UI style"** (`<runs-dir>/design/`), approved
   by Karen. The house rules this card follows: surface ladder, one brass hairline, BuilderSans Bold
   letter-spaced micro-labels, motion only as a colour/transparency tween.

## 4. The pattern adopted, and why

**One owner, two sizes, two palettes.** `ReportPanel`'s three private drawing functions move to
`src/client/Hud/BoarArt.luau` unchanged in what they draw, with the colours, the size and the
facing as arguments. The drive report then asks for its own 104 x 56 pale animal on a dark backing;
the hit card asks for a 164 x 70 **black** animal on a **bone plate**. One builder, one placer, one
projection -- the dispatch's own words, and CLAUDE.md rule 3.

**The black animal gets a pale ground.** Karen's reference is a solid black boar against a sunlit
wood. The house ladder is dark on dark, and a black boar on `#1C1915` is invisible. The card is
therefore a dark panel with a brass hairline, and the animal is printed on a bone plate inside it --
the way a paper hit card has one. This is the single deliberate deviation from the surface ladder,
and it is named here rather than buried.

**Per-pellet points ride underneath the existing report, never beside it.** `Hits.group` keeps
making ONE report per (shot, target) with `pellets = n` and the NEAREST impact as `position`; it
gains a `points` array of every pellet's landing place, bounded at 24. `HitLog.Shape.cardDots`
projects each through `Shape.dot`. The report's own single dot is still the nearest impact, and the
card marks it `lead`, so the card and the TAB report can be put side by side and the same mark
found on both.

**The facing is turned, not faded.** The report half-fades a far-flank dot because its rows have to
be the same way round to compare. One card of one animal does not: if most of the shot came in on
the far flank, the picture is mirrored and every dot is drawn solid, so what you see is the flank
that was hit. `Report.cardFacing` is that decision, pure and shared.

**A second hit replaces the card; it does not stack.** A buckshot volley into a sounder is two or
three reports inside a quarter-second and a stack would be three cards racing each other off the
screen. Karen asked for "where it landed" -- the shot you just took. The whole drive is still in the
TAB report.

**The fatal mark arrives after the card.** The animal enters DOWN on the Brain's NEXT tick, so at the
instant the card is drawn nobody -- server included -- knows whether the shot killed. Rather than
hold the card back for a tick, `HitLog.recordDown` sends one small `hitFatal` event naming the
animal and the shot's own clock, and the card marks its `lead` dot the way the report marks its own.

**The corner is the LOWER LEFT, above the kill feed.** The other three are taken at every width: the
minimap and the button rail own the top right; the ammo readout owns the very bottom right; the top
band is the objective line (y 8), the drive bar (y 44) and the compass (y 108, 360 px wide -- the
whole width of a 400 px phone). The card's bottom is computed from the feed's own two constants, so
the two cannot overlap at any viewport, and a client spec measures the rectangles.

## 5. The numeric targets

| Number | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| time from hit to card | < 0.3 s | one RemoteEvent, no yield: `recordHit` fires inside the `HitReported` handler |
| hold | 3.0 s | the dispatch's |
| fade | 0.5 s | the house style's only motion |
| card | 188 x 112 px | 47% of a 400 px phone's width, clear of the feed above and the compass below |
| plate / animal | 164 x 70 px | 2.34:1, against the animal's own 5.5 x 3 trunk (1.83:1) plus the legs |
| dot | 7 px, ring 1 px | the report's own `DOT_PIXELS`; the fatal one +3 and turned 45 degrees |
| dots per card | <= 24 | above a two-barrel buckshot pattern (9 x 2 = 18) |
| z-index | 14 | over the scope's mask (11-13), under the report panel (15) |
| plate | `#D9D2C4` | bone; the black animal (`#0A0907`) reads at luma 0.03 against 0.69 |
| dot / ring | `#D8251F` / `#F6F1E6` | Karen's red; a pale hairline so it reads on the black |

## 6. What this note does not decide

Whether the card should also appear for a hit by somebody on your own team (it does not: the server
sends it to the shooter alone), and whether a kill should hold the card longer than a wound. Both
are Karen's, after she has seen it.
