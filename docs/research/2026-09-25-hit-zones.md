# Research: hit zones and wounds (Milestone 1.5, Task 28)

Rule 1. Written before the code, after reading `docs/design/hit-zones.md` (Architect, Task 27) and
confirming every source that design cites from memory. The design says plainly that it could fetch
nothing (§15); this note is where its citations are checked, and **two of them were wrong**.

Date: 2026-09-25. Builder session, with network access for the source checks only.

---

## 1. What the system must do

Where a pellet or slug landed on a boar decides what happens to it: head and chest kill, the belly
and flanks wound it so it runs a short way and drops, the legs and hindquarters need several hits and
send it a long way — often over the exit line and away. Every hit must be *visible* (Karen's
playtest, 2026-09-25: she could not tell whether she was hitting anything), a killed boar must leave
a carcass, and the kill and the escape must both be published as events the score system (1.7) can
consume without this system knowing anything about scoring.

Numeric targets are in `docs/design/hit-zones.md` §11 and are not repeated here.

## 2. Sources

### A. Roblox assemblies and `WeldConstraint` — **confirmed**

<https://create.roblox.com/docs/physics/assemblies> ·
<https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/WeldConstraint> · first-party creator docs
(creator-docs is CC BY 4.0) · actively maintained. Fetched 2026-09-25.

Quoted from the page: "An assembly is one or more parts welded by a rigid WeldConstraint or connected
through movable joints"; "from a physics perspective, an assembly is considered a single rigid body";
"every assembly has a root part indicated by its AssemblyRootPart property … the part used to keep
consistent physics replication and network ownership"; and, on which part becomes the root, parts with
"Massless set to false (default) take precedence".

**Good:** the last sentence is the one that matters here and the design did not have it. Because the
three zone parts are `Massless = true` and the trunk is not, the **trunk stays the assembly root**, so
`SetNetworkOwner(nil)`, `AssemblyLinearVelocity`, the `LinearVelocity` mover and the `AlignOrientation`
keep working on the same part as before. Mass and inertia are unchanged, so `MAX_FORCE`, `MAX_TORQUE`
and `ACCEL` in `Boar.CONFIG` stay valid.

**Bad:** an anchored part welded into an assembly anchors the whole assembly; `WeldConstraint` needs
both parts already in the DataModel, so creation order matters.

**Adopted:** three massless, non-colliding, welded zone parts created after the trunk is parented.

### B. Roblox raycasting, `CanQuery`, `CanCollide` — **confirmed, with one correction**

<https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/raycasting> · first-party (CC BY 4.0) · maintained.
Fetched 2026-09-25.

Quoted: "You can exempt any BasePart from hit detection by setting its BasePart.CanQuery property to
false." The page says **nothing** about `CanCollide` affecting raycasts — which is the point: the
collision body and the queryable body are separate properties, so a hit box may protrude where a
collision box must not.

**Correction to the design's reading:** the page does **not** state that a ray returns the first
surface it meets. That behaviour is real (`RaycastResult` is the closest hit along the ray) but this
page does not say it, so the claim is carried here as engine behaviour the spec must *prove* rather
than as something documented. `tests/server/boar_zones.spec.luau` fires twelve real rays from
outside the boar for exactly that reason: if a zone part were nested inside the trunk instead of protruding,
that spec goes red.

### C. Stokke, Arnemo, Brainerd, Söderberg, Kraabøl, Ytrehus (2018) — **confirmed, and it validates two of the design's numbers**

"Defining animal welfare standards in hunting: body mass determines thresholds for incapacitation time
and flight distance", *Scientific Reports* 8, 2018.
DOI [10.1038/s41598-018-32102-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-32102-0) · open access,
**CC BY 4.0** · published 2018 (a paper; not maintained).
Fetched 2026-09-25 via <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137050/>.

The Architect cited this from memory and flagged it as unverified. The title, journal, year, author
list and licence are all as cited. It carries two calibrated formulas:

- expected flight distance `efd = 1.14 * M^0.73` metres, for body mass `M` in kg;
- maximum flight distance `mfd = 4.92 * M^0.73` metres, above which the paper attributes the distance
  to "marginal organ penetration or non-fatal hits".

**These are directly comparable to the design's two flight numbers.** For a wild boar at ~80 kg, and
at the repo's own 1 stud = 0.28 m (`docs/research/2026-09-24-boar-ai.md`):

| Quantity | Paper | In studs | `CONFIG.WOUND.FLIGHT` |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected flight, well-placed body hit | 1.14 × 80^0.73 = **27.9 m** | **100 studs** | `body = 120` |
| maximum flight, marginal / non-fatal hit | 4.92 × 80^0.73 = **120 m** | **429 studs** | `legs = 420` |

So the design's 120 and 420 — described in its own §15 as "a first guess … arithmetic against the
arena span, not measurement" — land within 20% and 2% of the published model for an animal of this
mass. The paper is about rifle hunting of moose, red deer and reindeer, so what is adopted is the
model's *shape* (placement, not only energy, sets the distance; thoracic hits incapacitate on the
spot). The numbers stay Karen's feel values.

**Bad:** rifle ballistics, not shotgun; metres of real animals, not studs of a 10-minute arcade drive;
and the paper is about welfare thresholds, not about how a game should feel.

### D. Hunting-association hit-sign ("Schusszeichen") guidance — **category confirmed, no single page**

Deutscher Jagdverband, <https://www.jagdverband.de/> · copyrighted web content; **no text and no code
taken**, only the observation · maintained as public guidance.

The design cites this as a category rather than a fixed page, and that is how it is used: the reaction
vocabulary only — bolt on any hit, slow while bleeding out, limp when the legs are hit (design §5.5,
§6.2). Nothing numeric comes from it. Not fetched: there is no one canonical page to cite, and nothing
in the build depends on it.

### E. `RaycastHitbox` — **the design's citation is wrong; corrected here**

The design cites `https://github.com/Swordphin123/raycast-hitbox`. **That repository path does not
exist.** The module is <https://github.com/Swordphin/raycastHitboxRbxl> (confirmed 2026-09-25: MIT
licence, stated on the repository page; ~70 stars; Rojo-compliant). The design's claim that it is "on
Wally" was **not** confirmed — nothing on the repository page says so — and this note does not assert
it.

The decision is unchanged and unaffected: **not adopted**. It solves swept melee / fast-projectile hit
detection; here the weapon already casts (`docs/design/shotgun.md` §5.4) and the boar only needs to
*be* hittable, which is three `Instance.new` calls with no dependency, no `default.project.json`
change and no Connect click.

### F. *theHunter: Call of the Wild* — behaviour precedent, **not fetched, nothing taken**

<https://www.thehunter.com/> · proprietary. Cited by the design for the player-facing expectation
(vital hits drop the animal, non-vital hits produce a long track). Nothing is copied and nothing in

### G. Inside this repo (rule 2 applies to internal patterns too)

`src/server/Boar/init.luau`, `Brain.luau`, `Body.luau`: an owner module that does nothing on require,
a pure decision module driven by an injected world, one module that is the only writer of Instances,
every number in one `CONFIG`. `src/server/Weapon/Hits.luau`: attributes as the cross-system data
contract — **verified in code this time**, not from a design: `Hits.targetOf` walks ancestors for
`Damageable == true` and reads `HitZone` from the **hit instance first**, falling back to the root,
which is exactly what child zone parts welded to the trunk need (design §2.3 made checking this the
first act of the build). `docs/design/camera.md` §10.1: a counter for the silent-degradation case,
here `unknownZoneHits` and `tallylessHits`.
**Adopted whole.**

## 3. The pattern adopted, and what is invented

Protruding, massless, welded, queryable zone parts on the existing physics trunk (A, B); a
two-quantity wound model — **damage** decides whether it dies, **flight** decides how far it gets
first — with the shape from the wounding literature (C) and the reactions from hunting practice (D);
all of it in a pure module inside the boar's existing owner, with no new system owner (G).

Invented, with the reason rule 2 requires: the **flight formula**
`flightStuds = maxFlight * (1 - clamp(damage - MORTAL, 0, span) / span)`. No source gives studs for a
400-stud arena, and the source that comes closest (C) models distance from body mass, not from a
damage score the game needs anyway in order to decide *whether* the animal dies. The whole invention
is a clamp and a linear interpolation between two numbers that C independently validates (§2 above).

## 4. Numeric targets

`docs/design/hit-zones.md` §11 is the single table and this note does not copy it. The two it now adds
a source for are `FLIGHT.body = 120` and `FLIGHT.legs = 420` (§C above).

## 5. What this note could not confirm

- **Source D** has no single citable page, and **source F** was not fetched. Nothing in the build
  depends on either.
- **`RaycastHitbox` on Wally**: not confirmed, and the design's claim is not repeated as fact.
- The raycast "first surface wins" rule is **not** in the page the design cites for it (§B). It is
  proved by `boar_zones.spec` with twelve real rays instead of being taken on documentation.
- Nothing about how `ApplyAngularImpulse`, disabling an `AlignOrientation` or a 0.06-stud protrusion
  behave **in this place** comes from a source: those are measured in the specs and the screenshots,
  and the review request says what was and was not observed.
